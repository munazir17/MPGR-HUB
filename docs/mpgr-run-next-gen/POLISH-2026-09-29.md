## 1. Buildings — more three-dimensional without moving anything

All of the following is drawn **on each block's own projected ground plane**, so
nothing floats, leans or shifts; block positions, spacing (`STREET_SPACING`),
lateral offsets, row gaps and the vanishing point are untouched.

- **Tone variants per block** — each block picks a precomputed front/section
  colour from a per-world table, so neighbouring masses read as separate
  buildings instead of one repeated panel.
- **Facade depth pass** (roadside row, nearest ~1100 units) — a sky-to-street
  shading ramp, recessed cladding bands with a lit sill, a lit near corner and
  a faint facade bounce below the contact line. This is the main change that
  turns a flat wall into a volume.
- **Aerial perspective between rows** — the outer street rows now carry their
  own haze offset, so the second/third rows read as *behind* the roadside row
  rather than as a second copy at the same distance.
- **Distant district band** — a new bounded band of hazy silhouettes fills the
  wedge between the street wall and the skyline, so the road recedes into a
  continuous city instead of an empty valley. The farthest half is a single
  hazed shape (cheap); only the near half carries faces and lit windows.
- **Continuity** — a soft haze band over the district, drawn *before* the
  street rows, melts district → skyline → sky into one gradient. The
  road-facing walls still draw last, so the earlier depth sort is preserved.

## 2. Runner — smoother stride, no timing change

`RUN_FRAME_START_MS`, `RUN_STRIDE_MS`, `runStrideFrame` and the per-frame
alignment table are unchanged, and the runner is still grounded flush on
`0.84H`. What changed is only how opacity is ramped across a pose change:

- `runStrideLead(phase)` returns 0 while a pose is settled and ramps to 1 at
  the boundary with a smoothstep. Outside the 62 ms window the runner is drawn
  as a single sprite exactly as before; inside it the outgoing pose fades out as
  the incoming pose fades in, and the two opacities sum to the sprite opacity.
  Because the ramp is zero-rate at both ends and complete exactly at the
  boundary, the pose change has no visible step.
- **Measured** (390×844, runner crop, 8 ms steps across a full stride): the
  worst per-frame change at a pose boundary fell from **16.0 to 2.4** mean
  pixel levels, and the worst step anywhere in the stride from **16.0 to 3.9**
  (units = mean absolute per-pixel change, 0–255).
- Ground contact: the contact shadow gained a soft penumbra that fades with the
  jump, so the runner reads as planted rather than decaled. No blur filter is
  used — `shadowBlur` stays 0 (existing test still asserts this).

## 3. Road + environment

- **Pavement shading** — the kerb band is now lit on its inner third and shaded
  against the facades, so the shoulder reads as pavement meeting the road.
- **Pavement joints / kerb-top highlight** — world-locked, projected through the
  same depth scale, bounded at 9 joints per side.
- Road geometry, lane markings, rails, chevrons, props, skyline, sky plate and
  horizon line are unchanged.

## 4. Performance

Paired same-host native software-raster comparison against the #63/#67 tip
(`53822ef`), populated fixture (3 obstacles, 9 coins, 1 power-up), alternating
order, forced raster flush. **Not device FPS.**

| World | 390×844 | 768×1024 | 1280×720 | 1920×1080 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| City | +7.1% | +5.8% | +6.0% | +9.7% |
| Ice | +9.0% | +6.9% | +5.3% | +3.8% |
| Desert | +5.7% | +12.3% | +3.5% | +7.5% |

Median **+6.5%**. The additions are flat, untextured fills (one gradient, no
`clip()`, no `shadowBlur`, no per-frame allocation); the software backend used
here is the least favourable case for them, while a GPU-composited browser
handles solid fills far more cheaply. Four separate trims were measured during
the pass (near-depth pass restricted to the roadside row, pavements 18→9 joints
and 3→2 quads, district 22→10 slots with a fog-only far half); the numbers above
are after all of them. No new per-frame garbage, no per-frame filters, no
texture uploads.

## 5. Unused-asset audit

Provenance scan of all 116 files under `public/games/mpgr-run` (exact filename,
stem, and path-form search plus a semantic search for the screen/pad art).

- **Deleted (11)** — the zero-content placeholder files literally named `...`
  / `....` in `checkpoints/`, `effects/`, `collectibles/`, `environment/`,
  `environment/city/`, `obstacles/`, `pads/`, `portal/`, `powerups/`,
  `screen/`, `ui/`. Each is exactly one newline byte, is not a valid image, and
  is referenced by no code path, test, script, config or document. The two
  keepers in `environment/space/` and `environment/volcanic/` were retained so
  those empty world folders stay in the repository.
- **Kept, confirmed referenced at runtime** — every sprite in
  `ALL_SPRITE_PATHS` / `CRITICAL_SPRITE_PATHS`, the road material, facades,
  sky plates, sign/banner atlases, cutouts and the audio track.
- **Kept, referenced only by tests/docs that pin them as intentional masters** —
  `portal/` (4), `screen/` (11), `pads/` (2),
  `effects/mpgr-run-level-complete`, `effects/mpgr-run-powerup-collection`, the
  legacy `*-side*`/`*-skyline` cutouts and the full-size HUD originals. No
  artwork was deleted: `run-assets.files.test.ts` explicitly asserts these stay,
  and the earlier asset-optimization audit records the same decision.
